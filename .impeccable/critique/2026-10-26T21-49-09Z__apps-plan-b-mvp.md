## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** Partly authored for this product.

Three things could only belong to Reroute:
- the Journey disrupted? screen: a red-tinted room, one 64 px "Get me there", then a 52 px arrival time and one sentence of reason;
- the option card's arrival box with its buffer word;
- the detail sheet's "Your limits" checklist.

Those three give a deadline-first, explainable comparison a real form. The rest could belong to any product:
- **Home** is a generic dark card feed: hero, call to action, weather, alerts, refresh, impact, rides, install, share.
- **The Impact Dashboard** is a fitness-app template: hero KPI, progress ring, streak squares, bar charts.
- **Every section** is the same rounded card with an eyebrow label, so a disruption, a weather reading and a CO₂ total carry equal weight.

The product's distinct idea, arriving before a deadline, has no visual form of its own:
- There is no deadline line or buffer bar on the cards.
- On the form, the deadline is the one optional field, behind a date-and-time picker.
- It doesn't appear at all on the Journey disrupted? answer.

Meanwhile the green CO₂ strip is the biggest coloured block on every card, louder than the lateness word. The visual priority runs against Product Principle 1 (Arrival first).

## Overall Impression

The system is disciplined: one column, one indigo action colour, labelled simulations, and a "why it doesn't fit" checklist that really shows the reasoning. But at the moments the product exists for, it contradicts its own principles:
- The results bury the options that would arrive on time under late buses.
- The Journey disrupted? answer promises "fastest" and delivers "least effort" without showing the deadline.
- The dashboard turns one planned bus trip into a streak and "100% not driving".

**Biggest opportunity:** give the deadline a visual form, and make every ranking, badge and headline answer "will I make it?" before "how green is it?".

## What's Working

1. **The detail sheet's "Your limits" checklist.**
   - It shows Deadline ✕ 8 min late, Cost ✓ $0.50, Walking ✓ 11 min, Live service ✕ Alert, then "Check before choosing · Simulated".
   - Unsuitable options stay visible behind "See why it doesn't fit".
   - This is Principle 2 (Show why) made concrete. Escape closes the sheet and returns focus to the card.
2. **Simulated values are labelled inside the content, not just on a banner.**
   - Examples: "Translink alert · Simulated", "10 min late · Simulated", and "Demo mode: this bus disruption is not real."
   - Every CO₂ strip states its basis, for example "410 g for this trip, against 740 g driving alone". That is Principle 3 (Say where each number comes from).
3. **The Journey disrupted? layout.** A 6% red-tinted screen with start and destination already filled, one 64 px red action, then one time and one sentence. The "one answer" promise holds in the layout.

## Priority Issues

**1. [P1] Results bury the options that arrive on time under late buses**
- **What:**
  - An unknown price counts as a failed cost limit (`live_routes.py:580-581`, applied at `:906`).
  - In a search with a deadline, that gave "0 of 7 fit your limits". The Uber, about 20 min early, and Drive, 16 min early, were badged amber "Check" and ranked 5th to 7th.
  - The default sort after a search is "Public transport first" (`app.js:2823`). It put four late buses on top, including the disrupted 427.
  - The first arrival time sat at 853 px on an 844 px screen, below the cost line, the warning card, the sort explanation and the CO₂ summary.
- **Why it matters:** this is the evidence-backed core (H-T01 and H-T03 accepted): deciding fast under time pressure. It breaks Principle 1.
- **Fix:**
  - Give "price unknown" its own state that does not fail the fit, for example "Makes your deadline · price unknown".
  - When a deadline is set, sort by deadline fit first, and apply "Public transport first" only among the options that make it.
  - Mark the earliest option that arrives on time.
  - Shrink the "No option meets…" card to one line naming what does make it, so the first arrival time shows without scrolling.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`, then `/impeccable layout`
- **Evidence:** confirmed in the browser at 390 px and in the code.

**2. [P1] The Journey disrupted? answer contradicts its own promise**
- **What:**
  - The label says "FASTEST WAY NOW" (`app.js:1187`), and home promises "the fastest working way there" (`app.js:611-613`). But `recommend()` picks the least effort among public transport that arrives on time (`live_routes.py:1000-1028`).
  - In practice it chose a 414 arriving 7:46 with 5 min to spare over a 427 arriving 7:39 with 12 min to spare.
  - The answer shows no buffer against the deadline (`app.js:1195-1200`) and never names the disruption it avoided.
  - With all public transport disrupted, it said to drive your own car. It gave no word about the disruption and no Simulated label, and Start directions sat below 8 one-minute driving steps.
  - **Loading:** while it searches, the button is a red shimmer with no visible words.
  - **"See other options"** loses the answer: getting it back costs another search.
- **Why it matters:** this is the highest-stakes moment in the product, and reassurance there depends on the answer agreeing with itself.
- **Fix:**
  - Settle the label against the logic.
  - Show "N min before your 8:00 deadline" under the time.
  - Add "Avoids the 428 · Translink alert (Simulated)".
  - Show loading text such as "Checking live services…".
  - Put Start directions right under the answer and collapse the driving steps.
  - Keep the answer in the back stack, and mark it in the options list.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`
- **Evidence:** confirmed in the browser at 390 and 1440 px.

**3. [P1] CO₂ outranks whether you'll arrive on time**
- **What:**
  - `lowestCo2()` and `co2Summary()` choose from every option, late or disrupted (`app.js:827-834`, `855-863`).
  - The summary named "The greenest option, 428" when the 428 was 5 min late, and again when it was the Disrupted option.
  - That disrupted card carried the solid green "Lowest CO₂" badge, the only filled badge on the screen.
  - On every card, the green CO₂ strip at 15 px/600 outweighs the 13 px lateness word.
- **Why it matters:** Principle 1 says CO₂ "never overrides feasibility", and leading with CO₂ rests on the untested hypotheses H-P14 to H-P18.
- **Fix:**
  - Award the badge and the summary only among options that make the deadline and have no disruption, or add "…but it misses your deadline".
  - Make the badge outline-only.
  - On failing options, reduce the CO₂ strip to one muted line.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter`

**4. [P1] The Impact Dashboard claims more than the data supports**
- **What, after one logged 4.6 km bus trip:**
  - "Equivalent to 1.8 km not driven", which reads as an error next to a 4.6 km trip.
  - "Not driving 100% of trips" and a "1 week" streak, from one trip logged before it was taken.
  - "Saved $0 vs driving" when the saving was unknown: `app.js:1550` turns an unknown value into 0.
  - The team's assumed 10 kg default shown as "your goal".
  - The empty state is four cards of zeros, with the real empty-state message at the bottom.
- **Why it matters:** this breaks Principle 5. PRODUCT.md says no measured CO₂ impact or mode-shift evidence exists, and this is a showcase surface.
- **Fix:**
  - Retitle it "Estimated CO₂ difference vs driving the same trips alone".
  - Show "Unknown" instead of $0.
  - Replace the km figure with "about the CO₂ of driving 1.8 km", or drop it.
  - Label the goal as a team default.
  - Call logged trips "planned", or log them after travel.
  - Lead with the empty-state message.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**5. [P1] Option cards and the Journey disrupted? answer are opaque to screen readers** (new beyond the audit)
- **What:**
  - Each card body is one button with `aria-label="Details for 427"` (`app.js:921`). That label replaces the arrival time, lateness, reasons and CO₂ that sit inside the button, and two cards announced the same name.
  - A failing card has two tab stops that both open the sheet: 14 stops for 7 cards.
  - The Journey disrupted? answer is drawn in place rather than as a new screen through `navigate()`, has no live region, and focus stayed on `BODY`.
- **Why it matters:** WCAG 2.2 AA is the confirmed target. The audit's planned fix in `navigate()` won't reach this answer, because the answer never goes through `navigate()`.
- **Fix:**
  - Remove the aria-label, or point `aria-labelledby` at the name, arrival time and status.
  - Give each card one tab stop.
  - Move focus to the answer's time and announce it.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`, as part of the audit's harden pass.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

**Cognitive load is high: 5 of 8 checks fail.** The failing checks are single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, minimal choices and working memory.

These decision points show more than 4 options:
- Home: 17 actions.
- Side menu: 11 rows.
- Next steps: 10 controls.
- Journey disrupted? form: 9 controls.
- Results: 7 cards and 6 sort chips.
- Journey disrupted? answer: 6 controls.

**Emotional journey:**
- **Lowest point:** the wordless wait after "Get me there".
- **High point:** the 52 px arrival time, which comes without the deadline, the buffer or the avoided disruption.
- **Ending:** it celebrates CO₂ (a green panel, a green button, a toast, a streak), even for a trip "outside your limits" and 3 min late. The peak-end moment rewards the wrong thing.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the deadline is Reroute's distinct idea, why is it the only optional field, behind a date picker, and missing from the Journey disrupted? answer?
2. Ten minutes from an exam, should an Uber that arrives on time at an unknown price ever rank below a late $0.50 bus?
3. What does Journey disrupted? know that Plan a trip with a deadline doesn't? If nothing, is it a mode or a preset?
4. What would the dashboard look like with only numbers the team can defend: no streak, no ring, no "not driven"?
5. Is demo mode for the presenter or the visitor? If it's for the visitor, why is it behind the logo, and why does it show Google API costs?
