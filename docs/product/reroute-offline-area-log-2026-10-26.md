## Decisions and deviations

- **Map data source:** OpenStreetMap instead of Google Maps, for licensing (above). The offline map is drawn by the app itself on a canvas, so no map library was added; it supports pan, pinch and wheel zoom and the subset of the Google Maps JavaScript API that the app uses, so the existing route maps, live directions and map picker work offline. Tapping places in the map picker does nothing offline (there is no Google place data); press and hold still drops a pin.
- **Public transport offline:** scheduled times only, with at most one transfer, from the downloaded days. Walks to and from stops use the OpenStreetMap walking network. Panic mode offline prefers public transport, as online.
- **Driving cost offline:** fuel uses the local average price saved with the area, or the price in Settings, and the card says which.
- **Legal pages:** the Terms, Privacy Policy and About / Data Sources pages describe the offline data, its storage on the device, what the server sends to Overpass and the ODbL attribution.
- **Service worker:** `VERSION` is `planb-reroute-8`; `offline.js` is part of the cached app shell; large offline downloads are not copied into the service worker cache.

## Known issues

- **Not seen with a real Google map:** the area box measurement, size readout and limit messages need a working browser map key, which the local checks did not have. Check them on the deployment.
- **Not tried on a phone:** offline detection on mobile networks, pinch zoom on the offline map, IndexedDB storage limits on iOS Safari, and live directions while moving.
- **Overpass availability:** both Overpass servers were overloaded at times during the checks; one download failed after about three minutes and the next succeeded. The app gives a clear message when a download fails.
- **Coverage:** coastline is drawn as a line rather than a filled sea, small islands in water areas are not cut out, driving has no turn costs, and offline public transport allows at most one transfer.

## What was checked

- Offline unit tests: 339 Python tests pass (`python -m unittest` in `apps/plan-b-mvp/`), including the area limits, the OpenStreetMap conversion (classes, access and one-way flags, junctions, simplification, sizes), the GTFS area extract (calendars and exceptions, trips partly inside the area, times after midnight, shapes), the endpoints, rate limiting and the background job handling. The offline routing, search, storage and map module (`offline.js`) has Node tests on synthetic data and in-browser tests of the map in headless Microsoft Edge.
- A review subagent read the whole change; its findings (including offline public-transport walks that could not be made in time, a map memory leak, rate limiting and thread use while waiting, and area measurement differences) were fixed.
- An end-to-end check drove the app in headless Microsoft Edge at phone size in dark and light: a real download of a 6 × 6 km CBD area, offline detection (event and service-worker paths), Use Offline Maps, offline Plan a trip with public transport, walking, cycling and driving results, the detail map, next steps and live directions at an emulated position, Journey disrupted?, the map picker's long-press pin, going back online, and deleting the area. Its findings were fixed.
